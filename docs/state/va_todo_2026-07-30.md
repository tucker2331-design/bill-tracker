## 1 · Data-quality guards: replace inference and magic numbers with identities

The owner's objection, verbatim: *"just putting 20+ minimum doesn't really give me a lot of confidence…
you are just guessing based on one or two sessions worth of data and even if we could use historical it's
not enough because the past doesn't repeat itself."* **Correct.** And "compare against recent behaviour" is
also weak — his second objection lands too: *"self relative counts sound like they could jump pretty easily
for normal things."*

Researched rather than invented. The industry frame is **Audit-Balance-Control**
([XTIVIA](https://www.xtivia.com/blog/etl-audit-balance-control-recommendations/),
[Alation](https://www.alation.com/blog/data-validation-techniques/)):

- **Audit** — log rows read / written / rejected at every step.
- **Balance** — *"explain the difference between source and target at each step."*
- **Control** — restartability and exception handling.

**Balance is the answer to FLOOR, and it deletes the number instead of tuning it.** The guard should not ask
*"are there ≥5,000 rows?"* — a question whose answer depends on the calendar. It should assert the
**conservation identity**:

```
rows_read_from_HISTORY  ==  rows_written  +  rows_dropped_as_noise  +  rows_rejected
```

Every term is already counted (`source_miss_counts`, `dropped_noise`). If the identity fails, rows vanished —
provable on day one of a session and on day ninety, at any size, with **no constant to calibrate**. This is
also strictly stronger than the floor: a truncation that still left 6,000 rows passes the floor today and
fails the identity.

**Apply the same lens to the other three guesses** (`derived-max 25`, `unconfirmed-max 150`,
`MINISTERIAL_MIN_SAMPLES 20`) before touching any of them. Some may have identities; those that do not should
be honest about being heuristics.

### 1b · The ministerial inference — the real fix is a bigger evidence base, not a smaller floor

**Established by probe, so nobody re-litigates it:** LIS publishes **no** meeting-vs-admin classification for
bill actions.
- `AdministrativeAction` is `True` for **37 of 3,912** codes and only for session procedure (Adjourn, Call to
  Order). It reads `False` for *Budget amendments available*, `False` for *Signed by Speaker* (clerical), and
  `False` for *Read third time* (a real meeting action). **Useless for this question.**
- The EventCode **numeric bands are not categorical** either — bands 0xxx, 1xxx and 2xxx all contain the same
  subcommittee descriptions, so the digits are not a category marker.

So the inference is unavoidable. **But the inference is not a guess about meaning — it is a FACT about
observed behaviour**: "this code has never carried a time or a vote." The only real question is *how much
data the "never" is drawn from*, and the answer today is wrong: **it runs over ONE session.**

**Fix: compute ministerial codes over the ARCHIVE, not the current session.** `Session_20261` alone holds
37,837 rows. The evidence base then grows every session with zero maintenance, `MINISTERIAL_MIN_SAMPLES`
stops being a load-bearing guess, and a rare-but-consistent code like H4002 (13 occurrences in one session)
accumulates until it clears any sane bar. **This is the sustainable answer; lowering 20 to 5 is the bandaid.**

**Blocked on:** the archive is a separate workbook the worker can read. No new source needed.

## 2 · Archive capacity — it WILL overfill, in ~4.5 years

**Measured:** Sheet1 is **29 columns**; `Session_20261` archived **37,837 rows** ≈ **1,097,273 cells**.
Google Sheets caps a workbook at **10,000,000 cells** → **9 sessions**, and Virginia runs roughly two a year
(regular + special).

The vault's claim that the archive "scales for years" is true and bounded. Needs a rollover-to-a-second
archive workbook, or a migration off Sheets for archived sessions, **before ~2031** — and the trigger should
be a measured cell count, not a date.

## 3 · The War Room across a bill's journey

The owner's framing is better than mine and replaces it: **the War Room lands ordered by soonest hearing.**
A bill is heard, drops back, and returns to the front when its next committee schedules it. His question:
*"when it comes to the front again, what data do we want from the last time, and where is it stored?"*

**Most of the answer is already right.** Positions and interactions are keyed by **bill** and **member** —
not by committee — so contacts made while a bill sat in House Ed are still attached when it reaches Senate
Ed. Nothing is lost by moving stages.

**What is missing is one field.** An interaction records *who, when, which bill, how it went* — but not
**where the bill was at the time**. Without it you cannot tell "we talked to Deeds while it was in House Ed"
from "we talked to Deeds last week before the floor vote", and the volunteer re-does work or repeats a
conversation.

**Proposed: add `stage` to `interactions`** — the committee (or "Floor") the bill occupied when the contact
happened, captured automatically from the bill's location at write time, never typed. Then the War Room can
show *"this stage"* by default with *"earlier stages"* one click away — **which is not a timeline**, because
it is grouped by stage and scoped to our own contacts, not a chronological list of LIS actions.

**Owner decision needed** before M4 renders anything.
