# Round 2 — Chief Lobbyist: rebuttal

## What changed my mind

1. **"Pivotality is exact arithmetic" was wrong. The mathematician and the statistician are right.**
   - Only the threshold is exact. Whether a given member is pivotal depends on correlated, uncertain votes.
   - A single member was decisive in only 3.9% of rooms, and a flippable target existed in 0.4% [panel: mathematician; genius].
   - I withdraw member-level pivotality. The pivot is the caucus, which is how Richmond works anyway: you move the majority caucus through the patron, the chair and leadership. [judgment]
2. **"Toss-up" measures our ignorance, not their movability (mathematician). Conceded.** "Find out first" was the right instinct; "persuadable" was the wrong word. [judgment]
3. **I overstated the plateau.** The twin result is an interval (75–86% on joint party positions from content [panel: mathematician]), and "no gain" means under ~0.65 points [panel: statistician]. My freeze call rests on product priorities, not a ceiling. It does keep the ~$4 bill-content read [repo] alive as one pre-registered test.
4. **The co-patron finding is now clean enough to put in front of volunteers.**
   - Co-patrons listed at filing match the undated list exactly (0 of 1,663 bills gained co-patrons after filing).
   - Subcommittee unanimous kills: 19.8% (n=425) with no co-patrons at filing vs 6.0% (n=517) with some [panel: moderator].
   - The statistician's dating leak is closed. It is still an association (statistician, mathematician), and I still say act on it: the cost is one phone call. [judgment]

## Where I agree

## Where I still disagree

- **Genius: "the lobbyist will keep the member as the unit."** I didn't; round 1 put the bill first. I disagree with "who to ask" *replacing* "who to work on." Volunteers exist to apply constituent pressure: ~12 points in a randomized trial ([Bergan 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1532673X08326967)), and the top-rated tactic with staff ([CMF](https://rollcall.com/2017/02/13/report-shows-untapped-power-of-constituent-advocacy/)). The member list stays, re-ordered: *ask*, then *reach*. [research + judgment]
- **Genius: informants will be candid.** An office tells a professional where the caucus is; a first-time volunteer often gets politeness. The informant should default to the org's **patron**, an ally who knows the count and wants to share it. That is lobbying as ally subsidy ([Hall & Deardorff 2006](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/lobbying-as-legislative-subsidy/AE4B5D8AB9C2487BB78C2A51BB53E03F)). The backtest should test "patron as informant" as its own arm. [judgment]
- **Genius (E): a randomized 2027 contact pilot.** No org will withhold contact on a bill it wrote. What an org can randomize is the *order* or *timing* of contacts, or contacts on its low-priority "watching" bills. Design it as a staggered rollout, not a control arm. [judgment]
- **Marketing: "95% on the most-confident 67%" as a selling point.** I side with the mathematician: that set is where volunteers need help least [panel]. "Tested and left off" can sit one tap deep for directors, but not on a volunteer's screen. [judgment]
- **Mathematician/statistician: a hierarchical model.** Useful plumbing for pooling contact-log answers, but don't build it before the log exists. [judgment]
