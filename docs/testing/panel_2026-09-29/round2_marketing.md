## Where I was wrong, and what changed my mind

**1. The "95% on the most-confident 67%" line.** In round 1 I called this "the 'break 95' a product can sell." The **mathematician** is right that any model can hit 95% on *some* subset, so the claim only means something as a risk–coverage curve [panel]. The mathematician also shows the confident set sits where volunteers need the least help, because the hard third is 72% other-party ballots [repo]. That is the owner's own "accurate when least useful" warning [repo]. I withdraw the line. Externally it becomes a curve on the methods page, always shown with its coverage, as the **statistician** asks [panel]. The **genius** says I "will want '95% accurate'." That misreads round 1: I said never to market comparatively and never to quote 86% alone. But my sentence invited the misreading, so the correction stands [judgment].

**2. The size of "94 in 100".** The statistician puts the design effect at 4.6 [panel], so the effective sample is about a quarter of the ballot count. The Likely interval is still tight, 0.936–0.953 [panel]. Any published calibration figure must carry bill-clustered intervals and a "2025 = validation" label. Only 2026 counts as a clean test [panel]. This hardens my round-1 point that the locked 2026 score is the one clean external number.

**3. "The model is at the ceiling."** The **mathematician's** Cover–Hart reading turns the twin result into an interval (other party 80% to 88.7%). It is not a ceiling [panel]. So I retire "matches the identical-bill oracle" and "stopping is justified by measurement" from any outward language. They are internal claims under dispute, and a skeptical funder's reviewer would find the gap [judgment].

**4. The Terms-of-service question is now partly answered.** The 2025 test ballots come from Open States, which is public domain [panel]. So a methods brief on historical calibration and the co-patron findings has no LIS §2 exposure [repo: `lis_tos_commercial_use.md`]. It still must credit Open States without implying endorsement, and the Open States terms should be re-read before publishing, because they can change at will and Plural, which runs Open States, is a competitor [repo].

**5. Co-patrons get stronger as a message.** The dating leak is closed: 0 of 1,663 bills in 2025 gained co-patrons after filing. On 2025 alone, subcommittee unanimous kills ran 19.8% (n=425) with no co-patrons vs 6.0% (n=517) with some [panel]. That is the cleanest number we have, drawn from one year of public-domain data. The phrasing stays "observed pattern", as all three analysts insist [panel]. The **lobbyist's** "asymmetric bet" is the right sales framing, because a co-patron costs almost nothing [panel].

## Where I agree, with a market twist

- **Lobbyist: lead with the bill, not the member.** Agreed. It also fits the market. FiscalNote's public forecasting is bill-level (Pre-Floor and Floor scores) ([FiscalNote](https://fiscalnote.com/blog/bill-forecasts-policynote-feature)) [research]. So bill fate is what buyers expect. Calibrated member triage sitting beside the org's own whip count is what nobody else offers [judgment].
- **Lobbyist: move "Tested and left off" to a methods page.** I now agree. It is a trust artifact for directors and funders, not for a volunteer on a Tuesday. The methods page and the funder brief are its natural home [judgment].
- **Genius: funders buy measured impact.** The genius puts it as "votes moved per volunteer hour", tested with a randomized 2027 contact pilot [panel]. This is the strongest grant narrative on the panel [judgment]. Two cautions:
  - The statistician's detection limits apply. One org over one session will be underpowered, so promise a pre-registered evaluation, not an effect size [panel + judgment].
  - An org rollout is blocked until DLAS signs [repo]. The grant ask must therefore cover the DLAS arrangement *and* the pilot together [judgment].

## Where I still disagree

- **Genius: the 96.4% informant figure.** It must never leave the building. The statistician notes it copies a party-mate's actual ballot, so it is a ceiling for a perfectly honest informant, not an expectation [panel]. Marketed, it reads as "just ask one legislator and you'll be 96% right." That is the Workado pattern of an unsubstantiated accuracy claim ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-approves-final-order-against-workado-llc-which-misrepresented-accuracy-its-artificial)) [research + judgment].
- **Mathematician and statistician: priorities.** Pre-registering 2026 and relabelling 2025 are cheap and essential. Cover–Hart intervals, estimating Bayes error from k-tuples, and hierarchical room models are sound, but they change no buying or funding decision this year. They belong behind the DLAS and pilot work [judgment].

## Revised top 5 (priority order)

1. **Pre-register and run the locked 2026 score once**, using the statistician's metric list with bill-clustered intervals [panel]. Publish only the label calibration, with coverage, plus a risk–coverage curve. Never publish 86% alone, a 95% subset claim, "ceiling reached" or "hardest third" [judgment].
2. **Reposition around the bill.** Lead with "Is it alive, and what can you change?": fate and room base rates, the quiet-death warning, and the dated co-patron finding (19.8% vs 6.0%). Member labels sit beneath as triage [repo + panel].
3. **Write the funder methods brief on Open States data only.** Include the tested-and-left-off list, attribute Open States without implying endorsement, and re-read its terms first [repo + panel].
4. **Build one grant ask that funds both the DLAS arrangement and a pre-registered 2027 pilot.** The pilot scores the contact log as forecasts and, with the org's consent, randomizes contact. It should be pitched through warm introductions, since Knight and Democracy Fund take no open applications ([Knight](https://knightfoundation.org/how-we-fund/)) [research + judgment].
5. **Adopt a claims policy now.** Named-member guesses stay team-only, as labels. Co-patrons are an "observed pattern". There are no informant or ceiling claims. Fix HB 1515's missing wording score before any demo [repo + judgment].
