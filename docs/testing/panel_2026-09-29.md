## Corrections the panel forced on earlier claims

1. **The identical-twin result is an interval, not a ceiling** (mathematician, via Cover–Hart): the best content-only
   accuracy on joint party positions lies between 75% and ~86%; other party 80–89%. "Bill content cannot say more than
   this" was too strong. The statistician adds sampling error (~±3 points on 734 pairs) and a dependence caveat
   (twins that see each other's vote agree more, which pushes the interval DOWN).
2. **97.3% → 96.4%.** The party-position figure included the member's own ballot; the leave-one-out figure is cleaner.
3. **2025 is a validation year, not a test year.** About 35 approaches were scored on it; only the locked 2026 score
   is a clean external number.
4. **"No gain" means "no gain of ~0.65 points or more"**, not "no gain". Step changes are ruled out; small gains are not.
5. **The two goals are arithmetic.** "Hardest third above 80%" ≈ 90.2% overall (the top two-thirds already run at
   95.3%). "Break 95" needs party positions right 97.6–98.5% of the time, against 86.8% today (mathematician).

## Where all five converged

1. **Stop hunting features for the ballot model.** Pre-register the metric list, then score the locked 2026 test once:
   bill-clustered log loss, Brier, accuracy, label calibration with intervals, room-outcome accuracy, and a
   risk–coverage curve by slice.
2. **Re-pose what is predicted** as three linked quantities (mathematician's S / Θ / O):
   - **S — the bill's fate:** never heard / stricken / killed on a voice vote / recorded vote / reported (a
     competing-risks model; most bills that die never produce a roll call, so a first-vote model sees a censored sample);
   - **Θ — each party's position** on the bill (84.6% of member errors live here);
   - **O — the room outcome.**
   Member labels become derived from these, never multiplied together into room claims.
3. **Put the bill at the top of the War Room:** fate, denominated base rates, and the levers an org can pull (co-patrons
   at filing, a bipartisan co-patron, a Senate companion, the patron committed to present). Members sit beneath as
   triage — "Likely" (70% of ballots, right ~94 in 100) tells a volunteer where not to spend a call.
4. **Retire "break 95" and "hardest third above 80%"** as goals; acceptance becomes paired, bill-clustered log loss
   pooled across rolling-origin years (2021–2025), z ≥ 3.
5. **Human information is the only route to a step change.** An office's stated position, scored as a forecast and
   pooled with the model, attacks exactly the party-position error. Needs a correlated room model to pool coherently.
6. **Co-patrons** can be shown as an observed within-patron pattern (not proven cause).

## Where they still disagree

| question | positions |
|---|---|
| "Who to ask" vs "who to work on" | Genius: lead with the one member whose answer best reveals the caucus. Lobbyist: keep the member list, reordered ask → reach; constituent pressure moves votes (~12 points, Bergan). |
| Who is the informant | Lobbyist: the org's own patron first (offices won't be candid with first-time volunteers); wants a patron-as-informant arm in the backtest. |
| Randomised contact pilot | Genius/statistician: needed to measure real persuasion (uplift). Lobbyist: no org withholds contact on its own bill — staggered rollout or low-priority bills only. All: only after a DLAS arrangement. |
| Hierarchical (correlated room) model | Statistician/mathematician: build it for coherence and informant updating, never as an accuracy play. Lobbyist: not until the contact log exists. Marketing: behind DLAS and pilot work. |
| Next data source | Lobbyist: stakeholder lineups first (House written-testimony trace, association position lists). Genius: aim interest-position data at S (Butler & Miller: lobbying predicts agenda progress, not committee votes). Bill-content read (~$4) kept as one bounded test by genius/lobbyist, ranked low. |
| External messaging | Marketing: publish only calibration tiers with coverage + risk–coverage curve from 2026; never 86% alone, a 95% subset claim, "ceiling reached", the informant figure, or named-member guesses outside the team. All agreed. |
